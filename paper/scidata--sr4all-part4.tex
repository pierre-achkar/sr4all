\section*{Technical Validation}

The automated extraction pipeline includes two internal verification stages, evidence-span alignment and LLM-based factual verification, that discard unsupported extractions before inclusion in the final corpus (see Methods). The quality of the extracted fields is assessed independently through manual expert validation of a stratified sample.

\subsection*{Sampling design}
The validation population comprises the 108,079 reviews processed by the extraction pipeline, of which 107,611 contain at least one non-empty extracted field in the final corpus. The unit of validation is the document: for each sampled review, all extracted methodological fields are assessed against the source PDF. Estimates are reported by field type.

The sample size follows the standard formula for estimating a proportion~\cite{cochran:1977}. At a 95\% confidence level ($z = 1.96$), a margin of error of $\pm 5$ percentage points ($e = 0.05$), and the conservative assumption $p = 0.5$, which maximizes the required sample size, the base sample size is
\[
n_0 = \frac{z^2 \, p(1-p)}{e^2} = \frac{1.96^2 \times 0.5 \times 0.5}{0.05^2} \approx 385.
\]
Applying the finite-population correction for $N = 108{,}079$,
\[
n = \frac{n_0}{1 + \frac{n_0 - 1}{N}} \approx 384,
\]
which is rounded up to 385 documents. The correction is negligible at this population size, so the sample size is effectively independent of $N$.

The operational sampling frame contains 88,584 processed reviews with an available source PDF; 19,495 processed reviews without an available PDF were excluded. The sampling design uses proportionate stratified random sampling, with strata defined by discipline and PDF file-size quartile, using random seed 42. PDF size in bytes is a proxy for document length, not a direct page or word count, and can also reflect images and compression. The selected sample contains 96, 96, 95, and 98 documents in the four size bins, respectively. The frame restriction means that the validation does not directly characterize processed reviews without available PDFs.

The planned sample contains 243 Medicine documents and 142 documents in other or unknown disciplines. Table~\ref{tab:validation-sample} reports the planned sample and currently observed coverage by discipline. Individual tasks do not retain their original size-bin membership, so completion by size bin is not reconstructed from the completed subset.

Because reviews do not consistently report every methodological field, per-field sample sizes vary with field coverage. The planned tasks contain non-null objectives in 355 documents, database lists in 351, and research questions in 68. The document-level sample-size calculation does not guarantee a five-percentage-point margin of error for every field. The assessed denominator and confidence interval are therefore reported separately for each field.

\subsection*{Annotation protocol}
Three annotators with expertise in systematic review methodology assess the sample against written guidelines fixed before annotation. The guidelines define correct and incorrect extractions and specify the treatment of boundary cases, including partial extractions and formatting variants. A shared subset of 80 documents is annotated independently by all three annotators to quantify inter-annotator agreement; the remaining 305 documents are allocated among them.

For each sampled document, two complementary assessments are performed. First, every non-null extracted field is compared with its verbatim evidence span and the source PDF, yielding a per-field correctness judgment (precision). Second, every field for which the pipeline returned no value is checked against the source PDF to determine whether the information is reported, yielding an estimate of coverage (recall). This measures the pipeline's stated trade-off between completeness and precision.

The shared subset is used to assess reliability; it is not the sole source of extraction-performance estimates. Each document--field contributes once to the performance estimates. An exclusively assigned document contributes its assigned annotator's judgment, whereas a shared document contributes a unanimous judgment or a label established by discussion. Conflicting shared judgments are not resolved by majority vote. Independent ratings are retained unchanged for the agreement analysis.

Inter-annotator agreement on the shared subset is calculated using nominal Fleiss'~$\kappa$~\cite{fleiss:1971} from the original independent ratings, before discussion. Extracted-value correctness (correct, partial, incorrect) and null-field presence (absent, present) use separate category spaces and are analyzed separately. Indexed Boolean queries remain individual items for agreement, rather than being aggregated as in the performance analysis. Final shared-document labels require discussion where the ratings conflict. The 305 exclusively assigned documents do not require three ratings under this design. Descriptive results by annotator and by shared versus exclusive assignment are retained to inspect potential rating differences, but do not support automatic correction of one annotator's labels or comparison of annotators on different documents.

\subsection*{Annotation agreement}
All three annotators completed the 80 shared documents. Across 717 extracted-value items, Fleiss'~$\kappa$ is 0.141 and mean pairwise agreement is 78.2\%. Across 228 null-field items, the corresponding values are 0.593 and 82.7\% (Table~\ref{tab:validation-agreement}). Pairwise agreement averages agreement over annotator pairs; it is not the proportion of items on which all three agree. Unanimity holds for 484 of 717 extracted items (67.5\%) and 169 of 228 null items (74.1\%).

Of the 233 disputed extracted items, 218 involve only correct versus partial judgments. Fleiss'~$\kappa$ is sensitive to category prevalence: for example, final study counts have pairwise agreement of 97.0\% but $\kappa=-0.015$. Both measures are therefore reported, without interpreting agreement as extraction accuracy. These agreement estimates use all independent shared-item ratings and do not depend on the unresolved discussion decisions.

Pairwise comparisons use unweighted Cohen's~$\kappa$~\cite{cohen:1960} and exact agreement on identical complete shared items, with no merging of correct and partial categories (Table~\ref{tab:validation-pairwise}). Mert and Pierre have the highest pooled extracted-value agreement, 83.8\% ($\kappa_C=0.213$), compared with 77.3\% ($\kappa_C=0.151$) for Arno and Mert and 73.5\% ($\kappa_C=0.108$) for Arno and Pierre. For the 228 null items, contributed by 77 shared documents, agreement is 87.7\% ($\kappa_C=0.729$) for Mert and Pierre and 80.3\% for each other pair ($\kappa_C=0.497$ and 0.550, respectively). These are descriptive comparisons, not tests of differences between annotators or evidence that the closest pair supplies the most accurate labels.

The pooled query result is strongly affected by item concentration. The 145 individual query items come from only 19 shared reviews; \texttt{W4406220777} contributes 126 items (86.9\%), of which 116 are disputed. On 105 items from that review, Arno assigns partial while both other annotators assign correct. Its comments describe queries distributed over numbered lines, embedded line labels, and mixed query fragments. These observations identify an interpretation and text-representation issue requiring discussion; they do not establish which rating is correct. Across all query items, mean pairwise agreement is 43.4\% and Fleiss'~$\kappa=-0.187$. Arno--Mert, Arno--Pierre, and Mert--Pierre query agreement is 20.7\%, 22.1\%, and 87.6\%, respectively. Thus the low pooled result should not be interpreted as equally pervasive disagreement across many independent reviews.

Post-hoc sensitivity analyses retain the original ratings and vary the weighting or aggregation only. Across the 572 non-query extracted items, Arno--Mert agreement is 91.6\% ($\kappa_C=0.306$), Arno--Pierre agreement is 86.5\% ($\kappa_C=0.237$), and Mert--Pierre agreement is 82.9\% ($\kappa_C=0.137$). Collapsing each document's query items to each annotator's worst original label gives 591 extracted document--fields and three-rater agreement of 86.5\% ($\kappa=0.209$); omitting the query-heavy review gives 587 extracted items and agreement of 86.7\% ($\kappa=0.204$). Equal weighting of the 19 query-bearing documents, by averaging within-document exact agreement, yields 79.5\%, 77.0\%, and 65.3\% for the three pairs in the same order. These diagnostics demonstrate sensitivity to the unit of analysis and do not replace the primary item-level estimates, resolve disagreements, or justify dropping an annotator. No inferential comparison or agreement confidence interval is claimed.

\subsection*{Reported estimates}
For each field type, strict precision is $C/(C+P+I)$, where $C$, $P$, and $I$ denote correct, partial, and incorrect document--fields. Partial extractions do not receive full credit; their frequency is reported separately. Indexed Boolean queries are aggregated within each document: all extracted queries must be correct for the document--field to be correct, and otherwise the worst judgment is used. An unresolved query excludes that document--field from the provisional estimate.

The available annotations support a conditional recall estimate, $C/(C+P+I+M)$, where $M$ denotes null fields judged present in the PDF. This assumes that every non-null field is actually reported in the source. The exports do not independently establish field presence for incorrect extractions, and an unsupported evidence span does not establish field absence. These estimates should therefore not be interpreted as unconditional recall without further presence judgments. In addition, the task generator created no null-field assessments for Boolean queries: 80 planned documents contain extracted queries and 305 have no query assessment. Boolean-query recall is consequently not reported.

Proportions are accompanied by 95\% Wilson score intervals~\cite{wilson:1927,brown:2001}. These intervals quantify uncertainty in the assessed units; they do not correct for incomplete sample coverage, exclusion of unresolved cases, annotator effects, or PDF availability.

\subsection*{Provisional results}
The three exports contain 182, 181, and 182 completed documents for Arno, Mert, and Pierre, respectively. They cover all 385 sampled documents: 80 shared and 305 exclusive, represented by 545 annotation records. No documents or requested item judgments are missing from the submitted forms; this does not establish that the supplied source material was sufficient for every assessment. At this stage, 292 individual assessment items remain disputed: 233 extracted-value items and 59 null-field items. These correspond to 177 document--fields after query aggregation, distributed across 70 shared documents. No discussion decisions have yet been applied. All performance estimates below are provisional and use resolved document--fields only; disagreement-related exclusion and limitations of the reference judgments may bias these estimates even though document coverage is complete.

Table~\ref{tab:validation-results} reports the current field-level results. Final study counts and objectives have strict precision of 0.973 and 0.963, respectively. Inclusion criteria, exclusion criteria, and keywords have partial-extraction shares of 14.5\%, 13.8\%, and 16.9\%. Year ranges and snowballing have strict precision of 0.960 and 0.941, but 87 and 96 null fields, respectively, were judged present in the source. These omissions account for 183 of the 299 resolved null-present document--fields. Research questions have only 67 resolved non-null assessments, giving a wider precision interval than frequently assessed fields. Boolean-query precision is 0.833 across 72 resolved document--fields; eight additional assessed document--fields remain disputed, and query recall is not identifiable from these tasks. These comparisons are descriptive, not tests of differences between field types.

\subsection*{Error analysis}
The error analysis distinguishes partial or incorrect extracted values from missed null fields. For extracted values, the current resolved set contains 213 partial and 36 incorrect document--fields. A further 118 extraction document--fields carry unresolved flags and are described separately rather than counted as confirmed extraction errors. There are also 299 resolved null-present document--fields and 59 disputed null assessments. Error categories are the annotators' selected labels, not independently verified pipeline failure mechanisms. Original comments, query indices, annotator identities, and document identifiers are retained in the audit. Each document--field is counted once within each category, including when several annotators selected that category or multiple indexed queries were flagged. Categories can overlap.

Table~\ref{tab:validation-errors} summarizes these categories. Truncated or partial values are recorded for 173 of the 249 resolved partial/incorrect document--fields (69.5\%). Comments describe incomplete lists of databases, keywords, or eligibility conditions, and omitted components of study counts. For example, comments for \texttt{W2089930396} identify an omitted PsycINFO search source; the evidence reported by the annotators lists ISI Web of Knowledge, PubMed, and PsycINFO, whereas the extraction retains only the first two. In \texttt{W2767010212}, the annotation for the initial study count notes an additional 1,251 references from reference screening and hand-searching that were not included in the extraction. These examples illustrate reported omissions, not independently re-adjudicated findings.

Wrong values are tagged in 31 resolved document--fields, and other errors in 28. Recorded comments also identify semantic or representation errors: for \texttt{W3023208672}, the keyword extraction includes the search-method description ``MeSH terms'' rather than only the terms used in the search. Query-specific comments describe loss of terms, quotation marks, or grouping syntax. In \texttt{W2804212020}, the annotator reports an omitted \texttt{eq5d} term together with missing parentheses and quotation marks. An evidence-mismatch example, \texttt{W4416930076}, is labeled partial with a ``hallucinated span'' tag because the cited span does not contain the query and the query string lacks an opening parenthesis. Such a tag is not sufficient to infer that the methodological field is absent from the PDF.

All four document--fields tagged with OCR corruption and all 14 tagged with ``value not supported by span'' are currently unresolved. They are therefore candidate error patterns for adjudication, not established frequencies in the resolved set. These dropdown-category counts are distinct from the comment-derived themes below: comments can mention a mechanism that differs from the selected category. Neither source identifies a causal pipeline stage without further verification. A document-grouped discussion report retains all 292 pending items with original ratings and evidence; a separate template contains blank decision entries for explicit adjudication.

\subsection*{Comment-based analysis}
To examine explanations beyond the dropdown categories, all 1,234 nonempty item-level comments were analyzed. They cover 990 document--fields in 286 sampled documents and comprise 1,096 distinct combinations of verbatim text, assessment kind, and field type. Comments accompany 385 partial, 43 incorrect, 67 correct, 341 present, and 398 absent original ratings. Repeated text was coded once within its field context, while all original observations, annotator identities, query indices, and resolution statuses were retained. Empty comments and separately supplied \texttt{found\_text} evidence were not treated as comments.

Coding was AI-assisted and exhaustive over this comment set. A non-exclusive codebook distinguished incomplete values, wrong values, evidence mismatches, OCR/layout, query syntax, normalization or field scope, source inconsistencies, source access or assessability, presence evidence, absence statements, supporting notes, unclear notes, and other substantive observations. A purposive AI-assisted spot audit examined 40 contexts across four field groups; follow-up inspection removed four unsupported OCR/layout assignments from comments describing semantic criterion grouping. There was no independent human recoding, comment-coding agreement estimate, or new PDF verification. The coded audit and codebook are retained with the analysis. Counts describe what annotators wrote, not independently established failure causes or corpus-wide error prevalence.

Table~\ref{tab:validation-comment-themes} counts each document--field once within each theme and current resolution status. Incomplete-value explanations occur in 172 resolved partial/incorrect extracted document--fields and 78 unresolved extracted document--fields; they also occur in 19 currently resolved non-error fields, showing that explanatory notes need not agree with the recorded label. Normalization or scope explanations occur in 72 resolved extraction-error fields and 37 unresolved extraction fields, and in 103 resolved null-present fields and 40 unresolved null fields. Themes overlap, so these counts cannot be summed to obtain an error rate. Comment absence is not evidence that a problem was absent, because explanatory detail was not supplied uniformly across labels or annotators.

The comments identify several distinct interpretation boundaries. Eligibility-criteria notes concern omitted conditions and qualifiers as well as splitting one condition into several criteria: for example, \texttt{W2122586864} notes that ``regardless of the dose'' qualifies a preceding criterion rather than constituting a separate one. Keyword comments distinguish actual search terms from indexing methods or publication keywords. Count comments distinguish records before and after deduplication, database-only retrieval from additional sources, and publications from unique studies. For \texttt{W4288748748}, an annotator contrasts 35 studies included in meta-analysis with 79 unique studies in narrative synthesis and 11 further publications. Such comments indicate that some disagreements concern field scope rather than simple transcription.

Source inconsistencies are mentioned in comments for 21 document--fields across 20 reviews, including 11 currently resolved extraction-error fields and seven unresolved extraction fields. For example, comments for \texttt{W4408204422} report 18 included studies in the abstract and flowchart but nineteen elsewhere. These are annotated reports of conflicting evidence, not a basis for inventing a single corrected value. Query comments additionally distinguish missing terms, grouping and field restrictions from line-number contamination and combined fragments. The concentration in \texttt{W4406220777} links these textual explanations to the agreement sensitivity described above.

\subsection*{Interpretation and limitations}
Source-access or assessability comments occur in 203 document--fields across 44 sampled documents. This broad theme includes unavailable main articles or supplements, supplied checklists rather than the intended review, and language or readability limitations; it does not mean that all 44 documents have an incorrect or unusable PDF. For example, \texttt{W2601615751} is described as a wrong-PDF/checklist case, and an annotator explicitly states an inability to assess Korean-language material for \texttt{W3129303378}. Of the null fields carrying this theme, 183 currently have a resolved absent label. Such labels may describe information not visible in the supplied material rather than verified absence from the complete review. Form completion therefore cannot be equated with an independently verified reference standard. These source concerns and their associated judgments require clarification before final performance claims; the existing estimates are retained transparently rather than silently excluding flagged records or changing their labels.

The observed quality profile supports field-specific uses of the enrichment, not a uniform claim of complete methodology reconstruction. Objectives and final study counts have high resolved-case strict precision, whereas eligibility criteria and keywords more often receive partial labels. Year ranges and snowballing show a larger gap between precision and conditional recall, so a missing value should not be interpreted as evidence that the method was not used or reported. Exact Boolean-query reuse requires particular caution because syntax matters, query coverage was not fully assessed, and item-level reliability is sensitive to one review's extensive search appendix. Discovery and assisted analysis with source verification are better supported than unattended reproduction of searches or analyses that equate null values with methodological absence.

Final performance and error frequencies remain contingent on discussion of shared disagreements and clarification of source-assessability concerns. Discussion does not change the original agreement estimates and does not eliminate possible annotator effects in the 305 singly annotated documents. The PDF-backed sampling-frame restriction, selective comment availability, conditional-recall assumption, and uncertainty in reference judgments also remain after adjudication. This analysis identifies those limitations without assuming a new annotation round or claiming that the current exports provide a gold-standard extraction dataset.
