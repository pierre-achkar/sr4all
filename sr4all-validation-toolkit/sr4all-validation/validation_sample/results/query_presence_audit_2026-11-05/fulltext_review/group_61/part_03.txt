Additional Interventions and Redosing
The success of fractured reduction without the need for 
further intervention was high across all groups. In the ket -
amine monotherapy cohort, the pooled success rate was 
86% (95% CI: 74–97%). This included 74% (95% CI: 
33–100%) in RCTs and 91% (95% CI: 83–99%) in obser -
vational studies, although the subgroup difference was not 
statistically significant ( P = 0.4099). Notably, combination 
therapy showed a near-perfect success rate of 99% (95% CI: 
98–100%) with no heterogeneity between studies (Fig. 3a). 
In contrast, in patients receiving ketamine combined with 
other medications, the success rate for fracture reductions 
without additional intervention was remarkably high, with 
a pooled proportion of 99% (95% CI: 98–100%). No het -
erogeneity was observed (I² = 0.0%, P = 0.7770) (Fig. 3b). 
Redosing rates were also analyzed, showing that 25% (95% 
CI: 6–44%) of patients receiving ketamine monotherapy 
required additional doses. When stratified by study design, 
RCTs reported redosing in 23% (95% CI: 7–39%) of cases 
and observational studies in 25% (95% CI: 0–54%), with 
no significant subgroup difference ( P = 0.9101). However, 
these estimates were subject to high heterogeneity that per -
sisted despite sensitivity analyses (See Supplementary Fig -
ure S6 for Redosing Rates) (Table 4).
Discussion
Procedural sedation involves the utilization of sedative 
agents to temporarily reduce pain and discomfort during 
brief surgical procedures [31]. This sedation technique aims 
to achieve proper sedation and pain control while maintain-
ing the patient’s capacity for autonomous breathing and 
preserving airway reflexes intact [32]. Moreover, the preva-
lence of procedural sedation in pediatric emergency depart-
ments (PEDs) has been on a rising trend in recent years [33]. 
The utilization of such sedative techniques when dealing 
with painful conditions in pediatrics, such as wound sutur -
ing, abscess drainage and bone reduction, has been con -
sistently reported to be safe and effective [ 34]. Hence, we
