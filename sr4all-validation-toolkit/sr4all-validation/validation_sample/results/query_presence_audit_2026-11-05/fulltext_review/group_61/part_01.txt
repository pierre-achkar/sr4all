Studies that focused solely on adult populations or proce -

Articles lacking disaggregated data on ketamine effects or

Study Selection Process
The screening procedure used a multi-phase, structured 
methodology. EndNote 20 was used to import all citations, 
and duplicate entries were eliminated. Two independent 
reviewers used predetermined eligibility criteria with blind-
ing maintained to filter abstracts and titles, and any dis -
agreement was resolved by consulting the third reviewer to 
minimize bias. After retrieval, full-text publications from 
potentially pertinent studies were evaluated for inclusion. 
The complete selection process was documented in a 
PRISMA-compliant flow diagram (Fig. 1).
Data Extraction and Management
A systematic, standardized process was used for data extrac-
tion to ensure accuracy and completeness. Two independent 
reviewers extracted relevant data using a standardized form. 
Extracted variables included study design, sample size, pub-
lication year, country, clinical setting, patient demographics, 
ketamine dosing protocols, route of administration, and use 
of adjunct sedatives (e.g., midazolam, propofol). Primary 
outcomes included the incidence of adverse events, spe -
cifically vomiting, agitation, hallucinations, hypoxia, apnea, 
and laryngospasm. Secondary outcomes encompassed pro -
cedural success rates, sedation and recovery durations, and 
requirements for redosing. Discrepancies in data extraction 
were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third 
reviewer. Missing or unclear data were clarified by contact-
ing the corresponding authors when possible. In cases where 
data were unavailable, imputation methods were considered 
following Cochrane recommendations [14].

Results
Characteristics of Included Studies
A total of 16 studies involving 3,167 pediatric patients 
undergoing fracture reduction with ketamine were included 
[15–30]. The study selection process is summarized in the 
PRISMA flowchart (Fig. 1). The studies were conducted 
across various geographic regions, including the United 
Kingdom [15, 19, 27], the United States [16–18, 20, 22–26, 
30], Spain [ 21], Iran [ 28], and Turkey [ 29], conducted in 
diverse healthcare settings, including emergency depart -
ments, pediatric trauma centers, and tertiary care hospitals 
(Table 1). The overall adverse event rate was 24% for ket -
amine monotherapy and 26% when used in combination. 
Ketamine monotherapy was associated with a 1–2% risk 
of respiratory complications, a 13% risk of vomiting, and 
a procedure success rate of 86%. Combination regimens 
reduced mild agitation but increased severe hallucinations. 
Fracture reduction success reached 99% with ketamine-
based combinations. High heterogeneity was noted across 
most pooled estimates. A detailed summary of the included 
studies and the participants’ baseline characteristics can be 
found in Tables 1 and 2, respectively.
Risk of Bias Assessment
Using The Risk of bias 2 (ROB-2) tool for assessment of 
RCTS, two out of six studies indicated a high overall risk 
of bias, two indicated some concerns and two indicated 
low risk of bias (See Supplementary Figure S1a for Quality 
assessment of risk of bias for RCTs using ROB-2 tool). For 
non-RCTS, ROBINS-I tool identified that one out of seven 
studies exhibited serious overall risk of bias, while six had 
moderate risk (See Supplementary Figure S1b for Quality 
assessment of risk of bias for non-RCTs using ROBINS-I 
tool). The three observational studies assessed with New -
castle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) demonstrated good quality, 
with no studies excluding based on the quality assessment 
(Table 3).
Outcomes
This systematic review evaluated a range of clinical out -
comes related to the use of ketamine, alone or in combi -
nation, for procedural sedation during pediatric fracture 
reduction. Key domains included adverse events, procedural 
performance metrics, and the need for additional interven -
tions such as redosing or airway support.
Risk of Bias Assessment
The risk of bias for each included study was assessed by 
two independent reviewers, with tool selection based on 
study design. For RCTs, we used the Cochrane Risk of Bias 
2 (ROB-2) tool. For non-randomized comparative stud -
ies, we used the Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies 
of Interventions (ROBINS-I) tool. Observational studies 
were evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS). 
Each domain was rated, and discrepancies between review-
ers were resolved by consensus or consultation with a third 
reviewer. The overall risk-of-bias assessments informed 
sensitivity analyses and interpretation of findings.
Outcomes
The primary outcomes are adverse event incidence, includ -
ing airway issues, cardiovascular consequences, emerging 
phenomena, and respiratory depression, while the second-
ary outcomes were sedation effectiveness, such as seda -
tion scales and procedure success rates; pain management, 
including procedure discomfort and pain ratings; and sub -
group results according to age group, method, and dosage.
Statistical Analysis
All statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.3, 
specifically the ‘meta’ and ‘metafor’ packages. For binary 
outcomes (e.g., adverse event incidence, procedural suc -
cess), pooled proportions with 95% confidence intervals 
(CIs) were calculated. For continuous variables (e.g., seda -
tion duration, recovery time), pooled mean values with cor-
responding 95% CIs were computed using inverse-variance 
weighting. When standard deviations were not reported, 
they were imputed from reported interquartile ranges or 
estimated using methods endorsed in the Cochrane Hand -
book [14]. We assessed statistical heterogeneity using the 
I² statistic and the Chi-square test (Cochran’s Q). An I² 
value above 0–40% was interpreted as low heterogeneity, 
30–60% as moderate heterogeneity, 50–90% as substantial 
heterogeneity, and 75–100% as considerable heterogeneity, 
prompting the use of random-effects models in cases of high 
heterogeneity (DerSimonian-Laird method). Fixed-effects 
models were applied in analyses with low heterogeneity. 
Pre-specified subgroup analyses were conducted to examine 
differences by study design (RCTs vs. observational) and 
sedation regimen (ketamine monotherapy vs. combination). 
Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robust-
ness of pooled estimates, including leave-one-out analysis 
and exclusion of high-risk-of-bias studies.
