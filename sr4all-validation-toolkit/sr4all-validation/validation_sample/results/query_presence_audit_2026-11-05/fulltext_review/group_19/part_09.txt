Prevalence
Few of the included studies were designed to assess the
prevalence of impostor syndrome, which varied widely from
9 to 82% largely depending on the screening tool and cutoff
used to assess symptoms. For example, Chae et al. 18 found
that the prevalence of impostor syndrome among 654 Korean
Catholics varied from 24% using a CIPS cutoff of 62 to 39%
using a cutoff of 58. We suspect that the included literature on
the prevalence of impostor syndrome may be subject to pub-
lication bias (i.e., the tendency of journals to publish studies
with positive findings rather than negative findings) since all
of the included studies reported some participants endorsing
impostor feelings.
