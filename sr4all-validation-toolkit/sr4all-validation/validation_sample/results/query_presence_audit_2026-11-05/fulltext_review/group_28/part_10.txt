Clinical Outcomes The DIVERGE study in the DBS group contributed 42.9% of the whole group. BRS group reported six cardiac deaths (0.67% vs. 1.01%). Two of the patients who died in the BRS group were confirmed to have stopped dual an-tiplatelet therapy against medical advice. BRS patients reported less myocardial infarction (1.89% vs. 3.2%) but higher stent thrombosis incidence (1.97% vs. 0.57%). Overall, DBS patients suffered more MACE (6.73% vs. 5.84%) (Tables 6 and 7).  A meta-analysis was attempted for MI, TLR, MACE, CD, and ST, which showed no statistically significant differ-ence between the two treatment groups in all tested out-comes. Meta-analysis failed for cardiac death due to the low number of cardiac deaths, which made the statistical analysis unfeasible (Fig. 2). 4. DISCUSSION 4.1. Patients’ Characteristics The five years’ difference in age between the DBS (65yrs) and BRS (60 years) groups can potentially cause some difference in the results relating to the general status of patients as they age, although both groups fall under the same category of coronary risk based on the Framingham study [22]. In a study comparing 2-years outcomes between patients younger than 65 years and those older, there was no difference found in cardiac mortality and TLR [23]. The DBS group showed lower smoking rates and higher hypercholesterolemia rates, which in theory may affect the study results, but as the Framingham study showed, both smoking and elevated cholesterol profiles tend to have a lower effect on IHD with aging and no independent effects after the age of 65, which annuls the effect of these two spe-cific risk factors [22].  The BRS group reported lower levels of smoking, a family history of CAD, and dyslipidaemia, which may re-flect that this group, in general, had a more selective ap-proach towards the patients enrolled. Although with regards to major risk factors, such as diabetes mellitus and hyperten-sion, the averages were very similar, thus supporting the homogeneity assumption discussed in the meta-analysis.
