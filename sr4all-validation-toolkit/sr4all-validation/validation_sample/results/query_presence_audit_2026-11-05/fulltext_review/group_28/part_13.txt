Capodanno et al. discussed the subject of scaffold thrombosis, especially at bifurcations, where scaffold throm-bosis carries ten times the risk of in-hospital mortality com-pared to scaffold thrombosis in non-bifurcation lesions; thus, recognising the potential of BRS, Capodanno recommends avoiding the use of BRS in bifurcation lesions altogether [31, 32]. 4.4. Limitations The lack of randomised controlled trials is one of the main challenges in this research. The use of non-randomised trials is uncommon in systematic reviews despite its repre-sentativeness of real-world conditions and outcomes. There is a clear risk of bias at the outcome level due to the different methodologies implemented in the studies regarding patient selection, as some studies enrolled patients and others re-cruited from registries. Attention was given to enrolment from the same registry and the time of enrolment; subse-quent removal of studies was made when a clear probability of duplication existed [22, 33]. Moreover, the rate of drop-out in the BRS group was remarkable, and that could affect the validity even though reporting on the reasons for drop-out was made. Moreover, the review was conducted by one reviewer, followed by the revision of two appraisers, but the effect of this might be limited based on the type of collected data, which is numerical, objective, and standardised (Table 1).  Finally, the design of a systematic comparative review has not yet been researched in-depth, and it may cause a lim-itation in comparability and reproducibility. Although it is a good design to reflect real-world conditions, it is still evolv-ing and not completely regulated.   CONCLUSION Patients who had coronary bifurcation lesions and were treated with bioresorbable scaffolds displayed statistically similar outcomes to those treated with dedicated bifurcation lesions, with a tendency for BRS to have a lower incidence of MI, MACE, CD, and a higher incidence of stent thrombosis. Further randomised controlled trials are recommended to deeply investigate this area. Moreover, the inclusion of bifur-cation lesions in major studies is advised as they constitute 20% of all coronary lesions.  While the risk of stent throm-bosis is a major issue occurring with scaffolds, new models with thinner struts provide hope for a better ST profile.
