HOUSING INSECURITY AND MENTAL HEALTH AMONG RENTERS 1 
 
Abstract 23 
Adverse social and economic conditions negatively impact mental health and well-being. 24 
The present systematic review is the first to investigate the association between housing 25 
insecurity and mental health outcomes among renters, with a focus on housing affordability 26 
and instability. We followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and 27 
Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. A comprehensive search was conducted in December 28 
2022 across four databases (MEDLINE, PsycINFO, Web of Science, and ASSIA). 29 
Quantitative studies from OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and 30 
Development) member countries were eligible for inclusion if they investigated housing 31 
insecurity by examining at least one independent  variable related to housing affordability 32 
and/or instability, and included at least one mental health-related outcome among adult 33 
renters. Due to heterogeneity of the identified studies, we performed a narrative synthesis. 34 
Twenty-two studies met the inclusion criteria, of which 14 applied a longitudinal design, five 35 
a cross-sectional design, and three a quasi-experimental design. Among the nine studies 36 
examining housing affordability, six reported significant associations between unaffordable 37 
rent and poor mental health in low-income rent ers. Regarding housing instability, 12 out of 38 
14 studies reported significant associations between unstable housing and renters’ mental 39 
health issues. Measures of housing insecurity varied, with rent-to-income ratio and forced 40 
moves being most commonly employed. Mental health outcomes focused primarily on 41 
overall mental health, well-being, and depre ssive symptoms, while few studies explored 42 
other mental health outcomes. The findings suggest that experiencing unaffordable or 43 
unstable housing has a negative impact on renter s’ overall mental health and depressive 44 
symptoms. Housing insecurity poses a significant challenge for renters in OECD countries, 45 
highlighting the need for policymakers to implement supportive housing policies and tenure 46 
protection measures in order to improve r enters’ housing security and ultimately public 47 
health.  48

Background 50 
Social conditions and contextual factors si gnificantly influence the risk, development, 51 
and persistence of mental disorders and psychological distress. According to the World 52 
Health Organization (WHO), social determinants, encompassing the conditions in which 53 
individuals are born, grow, work, live, and age, contribute to severe health inequalities within 54 
contemporary societies (WHO & Calouste Gu lbenkian Foundation, 2014). Housing is widely 55 
recognized as a significant social determinant influencing mental health and contributing to 56 
public health challenges (Baumgartner et al., 2023; Compton & Shim, 2015; Lund et al., 57 
2018; Marmot & Wilkinson, 2005; Shaw, 2004; Suglia et al., 2015; Swope & Hernández, 58 
2019). Housing insecurity is a global phenomenon, which exposes individuals to various 59 
forms of challenges, including unaffordable housing, poor housing conditions, a lack of 60 
stable tenure, and homelessness (DeLuca & Rosen, 2022; Eurostat, 2022; Prindex, 2020; 61 
United Nations, 2020). Renters, and particularl y low-income renters, are especially 62 
vulnerable to these housing disadvantages (DeLuca & Rosen, 2022; Hulse et al., 2019; 63 
Routhier, 2019). Indeed, insecure housing not only compromises the basic need for shelter 64 
but also poses significant risks to mental health.  65
