Methods 166 
The review was conducted and reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items 167 
for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (Page et al., 2021) guidelines (PRISMA 168 
checklist provided in S1 Appendix). To synthesize the evidence, a narrative synthesis was 169 
chosen over a meta-analysis, due to the heterogeneity of the included studies regarding 170 
methodology and the applied definitions of housing insecurity.  The review was not pre-171 
registered. 172 
Eligibility Criteria 173 
To be eligible for inclusion, studies needed to be quantitative primary or secondary 174 
research published in English that investigated housing insecurity by examining at least one 175 
variable related to housing affordability, such  as housing cost burden or receiving rental 176 
assistance, and/or housing instability, su ch as frequent moves, forced moves, or 177 
overcrowding. Studies investigating housing programs or policies aimed at improving 178 
affordability and/or stability, such as rental assistance or ev iction prevention programs, were 179 
also considered for inclusion. Furthermore, studies needed to include at least one mental 180 
health-related outcome, such as specific mental disorders, overall mental well-being, suicidal 181 
behavior, or treatment for mental health reasons (e.g., medication for emotional conditions). 182

Science Core Collections, and ASSIA (ProQuest). These databases were selected because 199

Study Selection 219 
After exporting all search results into the reference management program Citavi 220 
(Version 6.14), duplicate records were remo ved. The initial screening process involved 221 
assessing the title and abstract of each record for eligibility. If a record was considered 222 
eligible or potentially eligible, its full text was retrieved and screened accordingly. Study 223 
selection was conducted hierarchically us ing a screening form, based on the research 224 
question and predefined inclusion and exclusion cr iteria. Exclusion reasons for full-text 225 
reports were documented and are provided in S3 Appendix. It was ensured that studies 226 
which used the same database were distinct, as they examined separate research questions 227 
with different mental health-related out comes and/or variables assessing housing 228 
affordability and/or instability. The screening process was conducted by the first author, 229 
without the use of automation tools. In cases of uncertainty, discussions with the co-author 230 
H.N. were held to reach a decision. Additionally, to ensure reliability, a randomized subset of 231 
10 % of the full texts ( N = 12) was independently screened by the co-author L.N. and the 232 
interrater reliability was calculated using Cohen’s kappa (Cohen, 1960). 233
