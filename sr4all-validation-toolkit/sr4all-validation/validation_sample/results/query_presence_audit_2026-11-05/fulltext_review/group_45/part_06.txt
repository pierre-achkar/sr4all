Conceptualization of Housing Insecurity Among Renters 117 
Housing insecurity is a multidimensional concept. Although definitions vary across 118 
scholars, commonly applied dimensions include housing affordability, quality, (in-)stability, 119 
safety, and neighborhood opportunities (Clair et al., 2019; Cox et al., 2019; DeLuca & 120 
Rosen, 2022; Swope & Hernández, 2019). Hence, exposure to housing insecurity refers to 121 
conditions and situations in which individuals face challenges related to these dimensions. 122 
Housing insecurity intersects with insecurities in other domains, such as finance, 123 
employment, family, and health, often creating a complex interplay in which these 124 
insecurities interact and mutually reinforce each other (Hulse & Saugeres, 2008). With 125 
regard to renters, secure housing involves finding and maintaining homes that meet tenants’ 126 
needs, and is influenced by factors beyond legal tenure, including societal context (e.g., rent 127 
regulations), the market (e.g., affordability), pub lic policy (e.g., rental assistance), cultural 128 
norms, and psychosocial dimensions of securi ty (Hulse & Milligan, 2014; Hulse et al., 2011; 129
