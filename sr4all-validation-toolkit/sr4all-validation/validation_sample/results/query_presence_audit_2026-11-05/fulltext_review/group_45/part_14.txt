Housing Affordability 457 
Among the nine studies examining housing a ffordability, six reported significant 458 
associations between unaffordable rent, more specifically rent burden and reduction in 459 
housing benefits, and low mental health. The majority of these studies (seven out of nine) 460

Notably, the studies with significant findings a ll took income into account, mitigating the risk 463 
of conflating the effect of (low) income and unaffordable rent on mental health (Baker, 464 
Lester, et al., 2020). In addition, previous  publications also suggest that the burden of 465 
unaffordable housing mainly affects low-income renter and homeowner households, 466 
whereas high-income households do not experience such effects (Bentley et al., 2011; 467

Housing Instability 487 
Among the 14 studies examining housing instability, 12 reported significant 488 
associations between unstable housing and poor mental health among renters. The 489 
significant associations were established through a diverse range of measures of housing 490 
instability, most commonly the risk of evicti on, forced mobility, and frequency of transitions. 491 
Several studies also demonstrated positive effe cts of stable rental tenure and state eviction 492 
moratoriums in mitigating these adverse impacts. The absence of a standard definition for 493 
housing instability is evident from the divers e measures employed in the selected studies. 494 
However, the fact that many of these measur es demonstrated a significant association with 495 
renters' well-being emphasizes the importance of including a range of exposure measures in 496 
a unified definition of housing instability (Cox et al., 2019; Routhier, 2019). This will enable 497 
future studies to delve more deeply into the multiple pathways through which housing 498 
instability appears to affect renters’ well-being. 499
