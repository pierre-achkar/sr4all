Housing Affordability and Mental Health 367 
Of the nine studies examined in this review that investigated the association between 368 
housing affordability and mental health among renters, six found that unaffordable housing 369 
was significantly associated with a decline in mental health. Five of these studies employed 370 
a longitudinal design (Arundel et al., 2022; Baker,  Lester, et al., 2020; Bentley et al., 2016; 371 
Elliott et al., 2021; Mason et al., 2013) and one a quasi-experimental design (Reeves et al., 372 
2016). One quasi-experimental study (Prentice & Scutella, 2020) reported an inverse 373 
association, while one cross-sectional (Chambers et al., 2015) and one longitudinal study 374 
(Rodgers et al., 2019) found no significant association between housing affordability and 375 
mental health. 376

Housing Instability and Mental Health 405 
Of the 14 studies that examined the link between housing instability and mental 406 
health, 12 reported one or more significant associations between unstable housing and 407 
poorer mental health among renters. Six of these studies employed a longitudinal design 408 
(Desmond & Kimbro, 2015; Kim & Burgard, 2022; Leifheit et al., 2021; A. Li et al., 2022; Park 409

significant associations (Pevalin, 2009; J. Tsai et al., 2021). 413 
When considering only those studies that specifically measured overall mental 414 
health, five out of seven reported a signifi cant association between unstable housing and 415 
poorer mental health. Specifically, Leifheit et  al. (2021) and Ali and Wehby (2022) observed 416 
a positive association between the eviction mora torium implemented in the U.S. in response 417
