From a clinical perspective, the findings highlight the importance of considering social 562 
factors in understanding and effectively treating m ental health issues. Health professionals 563 
could integrate screening questions for housing insecurity into the assessment, and remain 564 
aware of its potential impact throughout treatm ent, given its potential role as a triggering, 565

approaches that integrate mental healthcare with social work and legal support might be 567 
beneficial. The use of a multidisciplinary approach could include helping tenants who are 568 
experiencing housing insecurity to find affordable housing, access housing assistance, or 569 
navigate or resolve legal issues. Similar approaches have been successfully employed in 570 
homelessness prevention programs that align with  the aim of achieving “zero discharge into 571 
homelessness” (Gaetz & Dej, 2017, p. 60; Moritsugu et al., 2019). As part of a 572 
multidisciplinary approach, health professionals would have a unique opportunity to shape 573 
public discourse, drive research efforts, and inform policy discussions to advocate for 574 
transformative change in the cond itions contributing to social disadvantages (Compton & 575 
Shim, 2015). The findings of our review point to the potential benefit of providing affordable 576 
and stable housing options for renters, particularl y for low-income households, as a strategy 577 
to improve public mental health. The question of which precise housing policy measures are 578 
effective, particularly for the most vulnerable groups of renters such as low-income, disabled, 579 
or racially marginalized households, remains an area for further research and investigation 580 
(DeLuca & Rosen, 2022).  581

It is important to acknowledge some limitations of this systematic review. First, only a 583 
random 10 % of the studies underwent double scr eening, and similarly, data extraction was 584 
independently verified for only 30 % of the included studies. This may  introduce bias and 585 
increase the risk of missed studies. Second, this review focused solely on two dimensions of 586 
housing insecurity, i.e. affordability and instability, even though housing insecurity is a 587 
multidimensional concept that includes further challenges such as housing conditions, 588

Conclusion 599 
This systematic review is the first to specifically investigate the association between 600 
housing insecurity and mental health among renters, addressing a crucial gap in the 601 
literature. The findings indicate an associati on of renters’ exposure to housing unaffordability 602 
and instability with adverse mental health outcomes, including overall mental health and 603 
depressive symptoms. Based on the current fi ndings, health professionals might consider 604 
housing insecurity as a contributing and aggravati ng factor regarding mental health issues. 605 
Housing insecurity poses a global challenge for renters in OECD countries, highlighting the 606 
need for policymakers to implement supportive housing policies and tenure protection 607 
measures in order to improve renters’ housing security and ultimately public health. 608
